# Doping

How we turn pure, barely-conducting [[Semiconductor|silicon]] into something useful: sprinkle in a tiny amount of a different element. Even ~1 impurity atom per million changes the behaviour dramatically.

## Two flavours
- **N-type** — dope with an element that has an *extra* outer electron (e.g. phosphorus). That spare electron is free to move → carriers are **negative** electrons.
- **P-type** — dope with an element that has *one fewer* outer electron (e.g. boron). This leaves a **hole** — a missing electron that behaves like a mobile positive charge. Carriers are positive **holes**.

## Why it matters
Neither N nor P alone is exciting — each just conducts a bit. The magic happens when you put them *next to each other*:
- N + P → a [[Diode]] (a one-way valve for current).
- P–N–P or N–P–N sandwiches, or a gate over a channel → a [[Transistor]] (a switch).

Doping is the raw ingredient. Junctions between doped regions are the actual devices.
