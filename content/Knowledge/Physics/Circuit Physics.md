## Conductors
Conductors are made of atoms whose electrons have weak bond to their nuclei. When a bunch of metal atoms are together, they gladly share their outer electrons with each other, creating a swarm of electrons. A very small electric force can make the electron swarm move. Copper, gold, silver and aluminium are good conductors.

There are poor conductors, whose electrons are less prone to move or have a relatively stronger bond.

## Insulators
Insulators are materials whose electrons are tightly bound to their nuclei. Modest electric forces are not able to pull these electrons apart from their nuclei. When an electric force is applied, the electron clouds around the atom stretch and deform in response, but the electrons do not depart. Glass, plastic and stone are examples of insulators. Even for insulators, though, electric force can always be turned up high enough to rip electrons away - this is called breakdown. That's what is happening to air molecules when you see a spark.

## Semiconductors
Semiconductors fall between conductors and insulators. They usually act like insulators, but we can make them act like conductors under certain circumstances. The most well known semi conductor is silicon (Atomic number 14). 
Our ability to finely control the insulating and conducting behaviour of silicon allows us to create devices like computers. 
The atomic-level details of how semi-conductor devices work are governed by theories of quantum mechanics.

## Current 
Current is the flow of charge
![[Current-flow-Charge.png]]

Current is number of charges per unit time passing through a point in a circuit. 

> What causes current or these charges to move from in a direction?
> Charged objects move in response to electric and magnetic forces. These forces come from electric and magnetic fields, which in turn come from the position and motion of other charges.

# Voltage
Voltage is equivalent to Potential Energy. 

For a mass m, a change of height h corresponds to a change in potential energy, ∆U = mg∆h. 
For a charged particle q, a voltage ∆V corresponds to a change in potential energy, ∆U = qV.

Voltage in an electric circuit is analogues to the product of g * ∆h. Where g is the acceleration due to gravity and ∆h is the change of height.
