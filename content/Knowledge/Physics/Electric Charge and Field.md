# Electric Charge and Field

The starting point of the whole story. Charge is the property that makes electrons and protons push and pull on each other. Like charges repel, opposite charges attract.

## Field: the "why" behind current
A charge doesn't reach out and touch another charge directly. It creates an **electric field** in the space around it — an invisible "slope" that other charges feel. Drop a free charge into a field and it accelerates along the slope.

This answers the question left open in [[Circuit Physics]]: *what makes charges move?* An electric field, set up by a difference in charge (a battery, a power supply), pushes on the free electrons in a [[Circuit Physics#Conductors|conductor]].

## Connecting to voltage
- **Voltage** ([[Circuit Physics]]) is potential energy per unit charge — how much "downhill" a charge has to fall. High voltage = steep field = strong push.
- **Current** ([[Circuit Physics]]) is what results: charges actually flowing in response to that push.

So: charge → field → voltage (the push) → current (the flow). Everything above this note is just controlling *where* and *when* that flow happens.
